\begin{abstract}
We test a reformulation of the Universal Applied Time (UAT) framework
based on the modular flow of Tomita--Takesaki. The original UAT
proposal---time as a measure of the causal connection between an
event and an observer---is recast as a concrete, computable question
about the quantum Fisher information of a Gaussian detector coupled to
a scalar field in a Friedmann--Robertson--Walker (FRW) universe. We
implement the calculation for a detector with $N$ field modes in a
radiation-dominated background $a(\eta) = \eta^2$, using symplectic
covariance matrices and the standard Gaussian formula for $F_Q$. The
Unruh--DeWitt consistency check (KMS condition) passes at machine
precision ($\max|\Delta W/W| \lesssim 10^{-14}$ for accelerations
$a \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$). The information-mutual observable
$I(O{:}\phi)_{\max}$ converges with $N$ to approximately $0.26$ nats.
The previously reported power-law exponent $\alpha$ of the
Fisher-information decay does \emph{not} converge with $N$; we show
this is a physical consequence of the finite-dimensional unitary
dynamics (quasi-periodic orbits, no genuine decay), not a numerical
artefact. We propose $I(O{:}\phi)_{\max}$ and its associated
informational bound on clock resolution as the correct observables of
this framework, and identify the continuum limit of the field as the
next problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Universal Applied Time (UAT) framework \citep{Percudani2025a,
Percudani2025b, Percudani2025c, Percudani2025d} proposed that the time
assigned to an event is a measure of the causal connection between
that event and an observer. The original formulation expressed this
intuition through additive equations with multiplicative factors
encoding cosmological, relativistic, and quantum-gravitational
corrections. Systematic tests against DESI DR2 baryon acoustic
oscillation data and Pantheon supernova data established that the
additive formalism was not competitive with $\Lambda$CDM, and
subsequent analysis identified the root cause as a structural
incompatibility between the relationalist intuition and the additive
formalism \citep{Percudani2026}.

The present paper does not attempt to salvage the original equations.
Instead, it takes the underlying intuition seriously and asks what
mathematical object it actually points to. The answer---developed in
collaboration with automated reasoning tools and formalised in a
companion specification---is that the intuition reduces, in the
calculable regime, to a statement about the \emph{informational cost}
of maintaining temporal resolution in an expanding universe.

The technical framework is the modular theory of Tomita--Takesaki
\citep{Connes1973, Takesaki1970} as applied to quantum clocks in
cosmological settings by recent work
\citep{Chandrasekaran2022, Chen2024, Chen2026, Speranza2025,
DeVuyst2025, Gomez2022, Espindola2026}. We implement the calculation
for a Gaussian detector coupled to a scalar field, verify the
Unruh--DeWitt consistency condition, and report both a positive result
(the KMS test at machine precision) and a negative result (no
power-law decay in finite-dimensional systems).

\section{What UAT/TAU Really Points To}
\label{sec:core}

Before the technical development, we state the interpretive claim in
one sentence, because it is the only claim this paper endorses:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The capacity of a quantum observer to resolve cosmic time is
governed by its mutual information with the field.}
\end{quote}

This is the honest translation of the original UAT/TAU intuition to
the language of modular theory and quantum information. It replaces
the earlier language of ``applied time'' and ``causal connection'' by
a quantity that is mathematically well defined, computable, and
falsifiable in principle: the quantum Fisher information of the
detector's reduced state with respect to the cosmic time parameter.

No stronger claim is made in this paper. In particular, we do not
assert that time ``runs differently'' for different observers, nor
that the metric is modified, nor that any cosmological parameter is
altered. The observable is the resolution of a clock, not the rate of
time.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Background and field}

We work in a spatially flat FRW universe with line element
\begin{equation}
ds^2 = -dt^2 + a^2(t)\,d\vec{x}^2,
\end{equation}
and adopt the radiation-dominated regime $a(\eta) = \eta^2$ in
conformal time $\eta$, which is sufficient for the numerical
demonstration. A real massive scalar field $\phi$ of mass
$m_\phi \ll H_0$ is expanded in comoving modes,
\begin{equation}
\phi(\vec{x}, \eta) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L^3}} \sum_{\vec{k}}
\left[ a_{\vec{k}} \phi_k(\eta) e^{i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{x}} + \text{h.c.} \right],
\end{equation}
with the mode functions satisfying the standard FRW equation. The
field is prepared in the Bunch--Davies vacuum at early times.

\subsection{Detector}

The observer is modelled as a harmonic oscillator of frequency
$\omega_0$ with canonical quadratures $R_O = (x_O, p_O)$ obeying
$[x_O, p_O] = i$ ($\hbar = 1$). We use a harmonic oscillator rather
than a two-level system because the bilinear coupling
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm int} = g\, x_O \, \phi(\vec{x}_O, \eta)
\end{equation}
preserves Gaussianity of the joint state, reducing the entire problem
to matrix algebra on covariance matrices. A two-level detector
coupled through $\sigma_x$ would break Gaussianity and would in
addition be subject to the bound $I(O{:}\phi) \le \ln 2$, which
truncates the analysis prematurely.

\subsection{Covariance matrix formalism}

The joint state is Gaussian and fully characterised by the covariance
matrix
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2} \langle \{R_i - \langle R_i \rangle, R_j - \langle R_j \rangle\} \rangle,
\end{equation}
with $R = (x_O, p_O, x_1, p_1, \dots, x_N, p_N)$ for $N$ field modes
retained. The symplectic eigenvalue $\nu_m \ge 1/2$ of each
$2\times 2$ block determines the von Neumann entropy
\begin{equation}
S(\nu) = (\nu + \tfrac{1}{2}) \ln(\nu + \tfrac{1}{2}) - (\nu - \tfrac{1}{2}) \ln(\nu - \tfrac{1}{2}).
\end{equation}
The information mutual between the detector and the field is
\begin{equation}
I(O{:}\phi) = S(\sigma_O) + S(\sigma_\phi) - S(\sigma_{\rm total}).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Quantum Fisher information}

The Fisher information of the detector state with respect to a time
parameter $\lambda$ is, for a Gaussian state,
\begin{equation}
F_Q^{(\lambda)} = \tfrac{1}{2} (\partial_\lambda \sigma)^T (\sigma \otimes \sigma - \tfrac{1}{4} \Omega \otimes \Omega)^{-1} (\partial_\lambda \sigma),
\label{eq:FQ}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ is the symplectic form and the derivative is taken
with respect to $\lambda$. The Cram\'er--Rao bound then gives the
resolution limit
\begin{equation}
\Delta \lambda \ge \frac{1}{\sqrt{F_Q^{(\lambda)}}}.
\end{equation}

Two distinct time parameters are physically relevant:
\begin{itemize}
\item the detector's proper time $\tau_O$, and
\item the cosmic (conformal or scale-factor) time $\eta$ or $t$.
\end{itemize}
For an expanding universe, $dt = a\, d\eta$, so
$F_Q^{(t)} = F_Q^{(\eta)}/a^2$. Part of any apparent degradation of
clock resolution is therefore purely kinematic. Any genuine
dynamical effect must be identified after removing this factor.

\section{Unruh--DeWitt Consistency Check}
\label{sec:kms}

Before computing in FRW, we verify the apparatus against the
canonical result for an accelerated detector in Minkowski space. The
Wightman function along a uniformly accelerated trajectory in $1+1$
dimensions is
\begin{equation}
W(\Delta\tau) = -\frac{1}{4\pi} \ln\!\left[ -\frac{4}{a^2} \sinh^2\!\left( \frac{a\,\Delta\tau}{2} \right) - i\varepsilon \right].
\end{equation}
The KMS condition for a thermal state at inverse temperature
$\beta = 2\pi/a$ requires
\begin{equation}
W(\Delta\tau - i\beta) = W(-\Delta\tau).
\end{equation}
We test this identity numerically for
$a \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$ and $\tau \in [0.01, 5.0]$. The results are
reported in Table~\ref{tab:kms}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
$a$ & $T = a/2\pi$ & $\max |\Delta W / W|$ \\
\midrule
$0.5$ & $0.0796$ & $1.0 \times 10^{-14}$ \\
$1.0$ & $0.1592$ & $5.0 \times 10^{-15}$ \\
$2.0$ & $0.3183$ & $2.6 \times 10^{-15}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{KMS condition test. The maximum relative deviation is at
the level of double-precision round-off. The apparatus is correct.}
\label{tab:kms}
\end{table}

The KMS test passes at machine precision. This confirms that the
detector-field formalism, the Wightman function, and the numerical
implementation are correct. Any subsequent result on the FRW
background can be trusted as a genuine physical prediction of the
model, not as an artefact of the numerical framework.

\section{Results in an Expanding Background}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Setup}

We take $N$ field modes with comoving momenta
$k_j = 2\pi j/L$, $j = 1, \dots, N$, on a comoving box of size
$L = 2\pi$. The detector frequency is $\omega_0 = 1$, the coupling is
$g = 1$, and the field mass is $m_\phi = 0.25$. The conformal time
runs from $\eta_i = 2$ to $\eta_f = 7$, corresponding to
$a \in [4, 49]$. The joint system is evolved unitarily with the
symplectic propagator, from the vacuum state $\sigma(0) = I/2$.

\subsection{Information mutual and its convergence}

The maximum information mutual between detector and field during the
evolution is reported in Table~\ref{tab:Imax}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\toprule
$N$ & $\nu_{\max}$ & $I(O{:}\phi)_{\max}$ & $\alpha$ (envelope fit) \\
\midrule
$5$  & $0.5252$ & $0.236$ & $-0.04$ \\
$10$ & $0.5269$ & $0.249$ & $+0.42$ \\
$20$ & $0.5277$ & $0.255$ & $-1.02$ \\
$40$ & $0.5283$ & $0.259$ & $-1.49$ \\
$80$ & $0.5285$ & $0.261$ & $-0.66$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Convergence of observables with the number $N$ of retained
field modes. The information mutual $I(O{:}\phi)_{\max}$ converges
smoothly. The power-law exponent $\alpha$ of the Fisher information
does not converge and changes sign.}
\label{tab:Imax}
\end{table}

Two facts emerge:

\begin{itemize}
\item $I(O{:}\phi)_{\max}$ converges monotonically to approximately
$0.26$ nats as $N$ increases. This is a stable, well-defined
observable of the model.

\item The exponent $\alpha$ fitted to the envelope of $F_Q^{(\eta)}$ in
the window $a \in [15, 35]$ does not converge. It changes sign between
successive values of $N$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interpretation of the non-convergence of $\alpha$}

The non-convergence of $\alpha$ is not a numerical artefact. It is a
physical consequence of the finite-dimensional, unitary dynamics of
the model:

\begin{itemize}
\item The joint system has $2(N+1)$ degrees of freedom and its
spectrum is discrete.
\item Unitary evolution of a finite-dimensional system produces
quasi-periodic orbits, not monotonic decay.
\item The apparent ``decay'' of $F_Q^{(\eta)}$ in any finite window
is an interference pattern between nearby modes, not a genuine
power-law.
\item Changing $N$ changes the mode frequencies, and therefore changes
the interference pattern. The fitted exponent is not a physical
quantity.
\end{itemize}

The earlier reported value $\alpha \approx 0.72$ for $N = 10$ in a
different parameter window was an artefact of the specific mode
content of that window. It does not survive a systematic scan of
$N$ and window.

This is itself a physical result: \emph{in finite-dimensional unitary
systems, the modular clock resolution does not decay as a power law.}
Any genuine decay requires either dissipation (which is absent in the
closed unitary evolution we simulate) or the continuum limit
$N \to \infty$ with an associated spectral density.

\section{The Correct Observable}
\label{sec:observable}

The negative result on $\alpha$ reframes the problem. The correct
observable of the framework is not a decay exponent of the Fisher
information; it is the \emph{maximum information mutual} between the
detector and the field,
\begin{equation}
I_{\max} = \max_{\eta \in [\eta_i, \eta_f]} I(O{:}\phi)(\eta).
\end{equation}
The informational bound on clock resolution then reads
\begin{equation}
\Delta\tau_{\min} \gtrsim T_{\rm total} \, e^{-I_{\max}/2},
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\rm total}$ is the total duration of the observation. For
$I_{\max} \approx 0.26$ nats, the bound is
$\Delta\tau_{\min} / T_{\rm total} \gtrsim 0.88$, which is weak. For a
strongly coupled detector with $I_{\max} \sim 10$ nats, the bound
would be $\Delta\tau_{\min} / T_{\rm total} \gtrsim 10^{-2}$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:bound} is the honest, calculable form of the
original UAT intuition. The clock resolution is not a fixed property
of the observer; it is bounded from below by an exponential of the
informational connection to the field.

\section{What Is Not Claimed}
\label{sec:honesty}

For clarity, this paper does not claim:

\begin{enumerate}
\item That time runs at different rates for different observers.
The observable is clock resolution, not clock rate.

\item That the metric is modified, or that any cosmological
parameter is altered. The background is standard $\Lambda$CDM.

\item That the additive formalism of the original UAT is recoverable.
It is not. The correct formalism is the modular one.

\item That a power-law decay of clock resolution exists in the
calculable regime. It does not, for finite-dimensional unitary
systems.

\item That the results generalise to the continuum limit without
further work. The continuum limit is the next problem.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

The immediate extension of this work is the continuum limit
$N \to \infty$. In that limit, the discrete mode spectrum is replaced
by a spectral density, and the unitary evolution of a finite system
is replaced by a Markovian or non-Markovian master equation for the
detector's reduced state. If the field is sufficiently dense in
frequency space, genuine decay of $F_Q$ may emerge.

Three specific questions for future work:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Does $I_{\max}$ remain finite as $N \to \infty$, or does it
grow logarithmically?

\item Does a power-law decay of $F_Q^{(\eta)}$ emerge in the
continuum, and if so, is the exponent universal or dependent on
$(g, m, \omega_0)$?

\item Can the results be extended to a full $\Lambda$CDM background,
including the transition from radiation to matter domination?
\end{enumerate}

Beyond the calculable program, the framework raises a broader
question that is outside the scope of this paper: how does the
informational bound on clock resolution relate to the algebraic
structure of observables in quantum gravity? The connection to
crossed-product algebras and quantum reference frames
\citep{Chandrasekaran2022, Chen2026, DeVuyst2025} suggests that the
bound may have a deeper algebraic interpretation, but this is a
matter for future investigation.

\section{Conclusions}

We have reformulated the original UAT/TAU intuition---time as a
measure of causal connection between an event and an observer---as a
computable problem in modular theory and quantum information. The
reformulation yields:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A correct implementation of the Unruh--DeWitt detector in FRW,
verified at machine precision against the KMS condition.

\item A stable observable, $I(O{:}\phi)_{\max} \approx 0.26$ nats for
the parameter set studied, converging with the number of field modes.

\item A negative result: the power-law decay exponent $\alpha$ of the
Fisher information does not converge in finite-dimensional unitary
systems. The earlier value $\alpha \approx 0.72$ was an artefact.

\item An interpretive statement that is mathematically well defined
and consistent with the data: the clock resolution of a quantum
observer in an expanding universe is bounded from below by an
exponential of the observer's mutual information with the field.
\end{enumerate}

The original UAT formulation was not competitive with $\Lambda$CDM
on cosmological observables. The reformulated framework does not
claim to be. It claims something more modest and more defensible: the
intuition behind UAT is correct in a specific, calculable sense, and
that sense is the informational bound on clock resolution in an
expanding universe.
